## Discussion

### 1. Principal findings

In this integrative transcriptomic investigation, we characterized molecular alterations associated with breast cancer brain metastasis using bulk transcriptomic comparisons and single-nucleus RNA sequencing. Our findings reveal a coordinated transcriptional landscape characterized by enhanced mitochondrial energy metabolism, reduced expression of extracellular matrix (ECM)-associated and immune-related pathways, and substantial cellular heterogeneity within brain metastatic specimens. Importantly, integration of bulk and single-nucleus data provided complementary perspectives on these alterations, distinguishing tissue-level transcriptional differences from the cellular compartments in which ECM-associated expression was predominantly observed. Together, these findings support a model in which breast cancer brain metastases exhibit metabolic adaptation alongside heterogeneous interactions with their surrounding microenvironment, rather than a uniform transcriptional phenotype across metastatic samples.


### 2. Metabolic adaptation in breast cancer brain metastases

Our bulk transcriptomic analyses revealed a consistent enrichment of mitochondrial energy metabolism-associated pathways in breast cancer brain metastases relative to primary breast tumours. Oxidative phosphorylation, aerobic respiration, and electron transport chain-related processes showed positive enrichment across the discovery, purity-adjusted, and validation analyses, indicating that the metabolic signal was not restricted to a single analytical model. The concordance across these analyses strengthens the evidence for a metabolic transcriptional shift associated with brain metastatic disease.

The enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation and respiratory pathways may reflect altered metabolic requirements associated with tumour cells adapting to the brain microenvironment. Mitochondrial energy production supports multiple cellular processes, including biosynthesis, redox balance, and cellular maintenance, which may become particularly relevant during metastatic colonization and subsequent growth within a distinct tissue environment. However, pathway enrichment alone cannot establish increased mitochondrial activity, metabolic flux, or dependence on oxidative phosphorylation. The observed transcriptional pattern should therefore be interpreted as evidence of altered metabolic gene expression rather than direct functional confirmation of metabolic adaptation.

The simultaneous enrichment of aerobic respiration and electron transport chain pathways suggests that the observed signal represents a coordinated metabolic transcriptional program rather than an isolated increase in individual mitochondrial genes. This consistency across pathway categories provides a stronger basis for biological interpretation, although the overlapping gene membership of these pathways means that they should not be considered independent evidence of distinct metabolic mechanisms.

An important consideration is that bulk transcriptomic comparisons reflect the combined transcriptional contributions of tumour and non-tumour cells. Consequently, the metabolic enrichment observed in brain metastases cannot be attributed exclusively to malignant cells based on bulk data alone. Nevertheless, the persistence of this signal across analytical models and validation data supports mitochondrial energy metabolism as a prominent molecular feature associated with breast cancer brain metastasis and warrants further investigation at the cellular and functional levels.


### 3. ECM-associated transcription and tumour–microenvironment remodeling
One of the central findings of this investigation was the divergence between ECM-associated transcription in bulk brain metastases and its cellular distribution in the single-nucleus dataset. Bulk transcriptomic analyses demonstrated a consistent reduction in ECM-related pathways, including ECM organization, collagen organization, collagen metabolism, ECM assembly, and ECM–receptor interactions. These changes were observed across multiple analytical approaches, suggesting that reduced ECM-associated transcription represents a reproducible feature of the comparison between primary breast tumours and brain metastases.

However, the single-nucleus analysis revealed that ECM-associated gene expression was not uniformly distributed across cellular populations. Instead, expression of the examined ECM-associated genes was predominantly concentrated within stromal cells, with additional contributions from pericytes, endothelial cells, astrocytes, and other cellular compartments. Stromal cells exhibited particularly prominent expression of genes such as COL6A3, AEBP1, SULF1, THBS2, and LUM, supporting their role as a major transcriptional compartment associated with ECM-related activity in the brain metastatic samples.

The apparent reduction in ECM-associated transcription in bulk tissue should not, however, be interpreted as evidence that the metastatic brain microenvironment lacks ECM-associated activity. The bulk analysis evaluates transcriptional differences between primary breast tumours and brain metastases, whereas the single-nucleus analysis characterizes the cellular distribution of ECM-associated expression within brain metastatic specimens. These analyses therefore address different biological questions. The single-nucleus findings demonstrate that ECM-associated transcription remains detectable within brain metastases and is distributed across specific non-tumour cellular compartments, despite the overall reduction of ECM-associated pathways observed in the bulk comparison.

This distinction is particularly important because tissue-level transcriptomic signals are influenced by both gene expression within individual cells and the relative abundance of different cellular populations. Consequently, reduced ECM-associated expression in bulk metastatic tissue may reflect a combination of transcriptional changes and differences in tissue composition. Our analyses identify stromal cells as a prominent source of ECM-associated transcription within brain metastases, but they do not independently establish the relative contribution of altered stromal abundance versus changes in gene expression per stromal cell to the bulk metastatic phenotype.

Furthermore, the distribution of ECM-associated expression across stromal, perivascular, vascular, and astrocytic populations suggests that microenvironment-associated transcription in brain metastases is not confined to a single cellular compartment. Although stromal cells represented the most prominent ECM-associated transcriptional population, gene-specific patterns across pericytes, endothelial cells, and astrocytes indicate a more heterogeneous cellular organization. This compartmental distribution highlights the importance of resolving cellular sources when interpreting ECM-related transcriptomic changes in metastatic tissues.

Collectively, these findings reveal a distinction between the overall reduction of ECM-associated transcription observed at the tissue level and the persistence of ECM-associated transcription within specific cellular compartments of brain metastases. This distinction provides a more nuanced understanding of microenvironmental transcriptional changes associated with metastatic progression and cautions against interpreting bulk pathway suppression as uniform loss of ECM-associated activity throughout the metastatic tissue.


### 4. Cellular heterogeneity and stromal organization in brain metastases
The single-nucleus transcriptomic analysis revealed substantial heterogeneity in cellular composition across breast cancer brain metastasis samples. Although tumour cells represented the predominant population, the metastatic specimens also contained myeloid, stromal, T/NK, pericytic, endothelial, neuronal, astrocytic, and other cellular populations. The relative abundance of these populations varied considerably across samples, indicating that the cellular landscape of brain metastases was not uniform across patients.

The predominance of tumour cells provides a broad overview of the cellular composition of the dataset, but the presence of multiple non-tumour compartments highlights the complexity of the metastatic tissue environment. Myeloid cells represented a substantial immune-associated population, while stromal cells, pericytes, and endothelial cells contributed distinct microenvironment-associated compartments. The detection of astrocytes, neurons, and oligodendrocyte-lineage populations further reflects the diversity of cellular transcriptional profiles captured from brain metastatic specimens.

Importantly, the relative abundance of cellular populations differed among individual samples. This variability was particularly relevant to stromal-associated populations, whose proportions were not uniformly distributed across the cohort. The observed relationship between stromal abundance and ECM signature scores suggests that sample-level variation in ECM-associated transcription is partly associated with the representation of stromal cells. Nevertheless, the relationship was not absolute, indicating that cellular abundance alone may not fully explain differences in ECM-associated transcription between samples.

Our ECM signature analysis further identified a continuous distribution of ECM-associated transcriptional scores rather than clearly separated groups. Although stratification into high- and low-score groups facilitated comparative analyses, these categories were analytical groupings rather than evidence of discrete biological subtypes. Samples with higher ECM scores exhibited increased stromal representation, supporting the association between ECM-associated transcription and stromal abundance. However, the observed variation may also reflect differences in transcriptional activity within cellular populations, contributions from other ECM-associated compartments, or broader differences in tissue composition.

The gene-specific distribution of ECM-associated expression across stromal, pericytic, endothelial, and astrocytic populations also suggests that microenvironment-associated transcription is organized in a compartment-dependent manner. Rather than representing a single homogeneous stromal program, the observed patterns indicate variation in the cellular sources and relative expression of individual ECM-associated genes. This heterogeneity may be important for understanding why metastatic samples differ in their ECM-associated transcriptional profiles even when they share a common anatomical site.

Taken together, these findings support a model of breast cancer brain metastases characterized by substantial inter-sample cellular heterogeneity and a continuous spectrum of ECM-associated transcriptional states. The coexistence of tumour-dominated samples, variable stromal representation, and compartment-specific ECM-associated expression emphasizes the importance of considering both cellular composition and transcriptional activity when investigating the metastatic brain microenvironment. However, these findings do not establish discrete metastatic subtypes or demonstrate functional differences in ECM deposition, organization, or mechanical properties.


### 5. Integrating bulk and single-nucleus transcriptomics: complementary perspectives on metastatic biology
The integration of bulk and single-nucleus transcriptomic analyses provides a broader understanding of the molecular and cellular features associated with breast cancer brain metastasis. While bulk transcriptomics identified tissue-level transcriptional differences between primary breast tumours and brain metastases, single-nucleus analysis resolved the cellular composition and distribution of selected microenvironment-associated transcriptional programs within brain metastatic specimens. These complementary approaches enabled us to interpret the observed molecular alterations within the context of metastatic tissue heterogeneity.

A prominent feature of the bulk analysis was the consistent enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation, aerobic respiration, and electron transport chain-related pathways in brain metastases. In contrast, ECM-associated and selected immune-related pathways exhibited reduced transcriptional enrichment. The consistency of these patterns across discovery, purity-adjusted, and validation analyses supports their robustness at the transcriptomic level. However, the bulk dataset provides a tissue-level representation and cannot independently resolve the cellular origin of these signals or distinguish transcriptional changes from alterations in cellular composition.

The single-nucleus analysis complemented these findings by demonstrating that brain metastatic specimens contain diverse cellular populations and that ECM-associated transcription is predominantly represented within non-tumour compartments, particularly stromal cells. Additional expression across pericytes, endothelial cells, and astrocytes suggests that ECM-associated transcription is distributed across multiple cellular niches rather than being restricted to a single population. This cellular resolution helps contextualize the reduced ECM-associated transcription observed in bulk tissue and demonstrates that tissue-level pathway changes do not necessarily imply uniform transcriptional suppression across every cellular compartment.

The relationship between stromal abundance and ECM signature scores further illustrates the importance of cellular composition in interpreting transcriptomic differences. Although samples with higher ECM-associated scores generally exhibited greater stromal representation, the relationship was not complete. This observation suggests that sample-level ECM-associated transcription may reflect both differences in the abundance of contributing cell populations and variation in transcriptional activity. Nevertheless, our analyses do not fully disentangle these contributions, and the independent datasets do not permit direct patient-level correspondence between bulk expression changes and single-nucleus cellular sources.

Taken together, the two analytical approaches support a model in which breast cancer brain metastases exhibit a distinctive tissue-level metabolic transcriptional profile alongside a heterogeneous microenvironment characterized by compartment-specific ECM-associated expression. Rather than interpreting reduced bulk ECM enrichment as evidence of uniform ECM loss, the single-nucleus findings emphasize the importance of cellular context and sample heterogeneity. This integrated perspective highlights the value of combining tissue-level transcriptomic comparisons with cellular-resolution analyses to better characterize the molecular organization of brain metastases, while recognizing that direct functional and patient-matched validation will be necessary to establish the underlying mechanisms.



### 6. Biological and potential clinical implications
The combined metabolic and microenvironment-associated transcriptional findings provide several avenues for understanding breast cancer brain metastasis. The consistent enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation and mitochondrial respiration-associated pathways suggests that mitochondrial energy metabolism may represent an important feature of the transcriptional state associated with brain metastatic disease. Metabolic plasticity is particularly relevant in metastatic cancer, where tumour cells encounter distinct tissue environments and must adapt to changing metabolic demands during dissemination, survival, and colonization.

The brain represents a metabolically distinct organ with a specialized cellular environment. The transcriptional enrichment of mitochondrial energy-metabolism pathways observed in our bulk analysis raises the possibility that metabolic adaptation contributes to the molecular characteristics of breast cancer brain metastases. However, because our analyses are based on transcriptomic data and do not directly measure metabolic activity, substrate utilization, or mitochondrial function, the precise mechanisms underlying this enrichment remain unresolved. Further investigation using metabolic profiling, mitochondrial functional assays, and tumour-cell-specific analyses would be necessary to determine whether metastatic cells exhibit increased oxidative metabolic capacity or functional dependence on mitochondrial respiration.

Beyond metabolic alterations, the heterogeneous distribution of ECM-associated transcription across stromal, pericytic, endothelial, and astrocytic populations suggests that interactions between tumour cells and the surrounding microenvironment may represent another important dimension of brain metastatic biology. These cellular compartments may contribute distinct transcriptional programs associated with structural support, vascular organization, intercellular communication, and tissue adaptation. Our findings emphasize that microenvironmental contributions should not be considered interchangeable, as different cell populations exhibited distinct ECM-associated expression patterns.

The relationship between stromal abundance and ECM-associated transcription also has potential implications for interpreting metastatic heterogeneity. Samples with higher stromal representation tended to exhibit higher ECM signature scores, but the continuous variation observed across specimens argues against a simple categorical model of metastatic microenvironment organization. Instead, the differences may reflect varying degrees of stromal representation and transcriptional activity across individual metastatic samples. This raises the possibility that microenvironment-associated states differ between lesions, although their functional consequences and relationship to disease progression cannot be established using the present dataset alone.

Emerging research also highlights the possibility that metabolic adaptation in cancer may be influenced by interactions with surrounding neural components, rather than solely by intrinsic changes in tumour-cell metabolism. For example, experimental work published in 2025 demonstrated neuron-to-cancer mitochondrial transfer in breast cancer models and linked the acquisition of neuronal mitochondria to enhanced metabolic capacity and metastatic potential. This provides an interesting mechanistic context for investigating metabolic plasticity in breast cancer, particularly in neural environments. However, our study did not investigate mitochondrial transfer, direct neuronal–tumour interactions, or neural metabolic support, and therefore these mechanisms should be considered potential directions for future research rather than explanations of our observed metabolic signature.

From a translational perspective, these findings may help identify biological processes warranting further investigation in brain metastatic disease. Mitochondrial metabolism, stromal-associated transcription, and cellular interactions within the metastatic microenvironment represent potential research directions for mechanistic studies. Nevertheless, pathway enrichment and gene-expression signatures alone are insufficient to identify therapeutic vulnerabilities or predict treatment response. Functional validation, spatially resolved analyses, and independent clinical cohorts will be required before these findings can inform therapeutic strategies.

Taken together, our results provide a molecular framework for investigating breast cancer brain metastasis that considers both metabolic transcriptional changes and microenvironmental heterogeneity. Rather than suggesting a single dominant mechanism, the findings highlight several interconnected biological dimensions that may contribute to metastatic adaptation and warrant further investigation.


### 7. Limitations and future directions
Despite providing complementary insights into breast cancer brain metastasis, several limitations should be considered when interpreting our findings.
Dataset integration and patient-level correspondence
Our bulk and single-nucleus transcriptomic analyses were performed using separate datasets rather than patient-matched specimens. Consequently, the observed metabolic and ECM-associated transcriptional patterns in bulk tissue cannot be directly linked to the cellular populations identified in the single-nucleus dataset. Although integrating these findings provides a broader biological interpretation, it does not establish direct correspondence between tissue-level transcriptional alterations and cellular mechanisms within individual patients.
Bulk transcriptomic interpretation and cellular composition
Bulk transcriptomic measurements reflect the combined gene-expression profiles of multiple cellular populations within a tissue. Accordingly, differences between primary breast tumours and brain metastases may arise from changes in gene expression within individual cell populations, differences in cellular abundance, or a combination of these factors. Although purity-adjusted analyses helped assess the robustness of differential expression findings, they cannot fully resolve the contributions of individual cellular populations or eliminate all potential sources of compositional confounding. Therefore, pathway-level changes, including metabolic enrichment and reduced ECM-associated transcription, should be interpreted as tissue-level transcriptional differences rather than definitive evidence of cell-intrinsic alterations.
Single-nucleus transcriptomic limitations
The single-nucleus analysis provided cellular resolution of brain metastatic specimens; however, cellular proportions derived from single-nucleus sequencing may not accurately represent the absolute composition of the original tissue. Differences in nuclear isolation, cell-type-specific nuclear recovery, sequencing depth, quality control, and annotation may influence the representation of individual populations. Rare cellular populations may also be underrepresented, limiting the ability to characterize their transcriptional contributions comprehensively. Consequently, the cellular composition estimates and cellular-source assignments should be interpreted within the context of the analyzed dataset rather than as definitive quantitative measurements of metastatic tissue composition.
Interpretation of ECM-associated transcriptional signatures
Our ECM signature was developed to characterize sample-level variation in ECM-associated transcription, but it represents a gene-expression-based proxy rather than a direct measurement of extracellular matrix abundance or structural organization. The association between ECM signature scores and stromal abundance indicates that cellular composition contributes to the observed variation; however, the signature may also reflect differences in transcriptional activity within individual cell populations and contributions from other ECM-associated compartments. Furthermore, high- and low-score group comparisons were performed using an analytical stratification of a continuous score distribution and should not be interpreted as evidence of discrete biological subtypes. Direct characterization of ECM deposition, organization, remodeling, and mechanical properties would require additional experimental approaches.
Cohort size and biological heterogeneity
The number of samples available for the single-nucleus analysis limited the statistical power to characterize uncommon cellular populations and investigate more complex relationships between cellular composition and ECM-associated transcription. Although the observed sample-level heterogeneity provides evidence of variation among metastatic specimens, the available cohort may not capture the full spectrum of biological variation associated with breast cancer brain metastasis. Independent cohorts will be required to establish the reproducibility and generalizability of these patterns, particularly for the sample-level ECM-associated transcriptional states identified in this study.
Lack of functional and spatial validation
Finally, this study was based primarily on transcriptomic analyses and did not include direct experimental validation of the inferred metabolic or microenvironment-associated processes. The enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation-related pathways does not establish increased mitochondrial respiration or metabolic dependency, while ECM-associated gene expression does not directly demonstrate matrix deposition, organization, or functional remodeling. Similarly, cellular expression patterns alone cannot establish direct interactions between tumour cells and surrounding neural, vascular, or stromal populations. Future studies integrating spatial transcriptomics, multiplex tissue imaging, protein-level validation, metabolic assays, and functional experiments will be necessary to investigate these mechanisms.
Future directions
Future investigations could build upon these findings through several complementary approaches. Patient-matched primary breast tumours and brain metastases analyzed using single-cell or spatially resolved methods would help distinguish cell-intrinsic transcriptional changes from alterations in cellular composition. Functional metabolic characterization could establish whether the observed oxidative phosphorylation-associated transcriptional signature corresponds to altered mitochondrial activity or metabolic dependence. Spatial and protein-level analyses of ECM-associated genes could further characterize their cellular distribution, matrix organization, and relationship to metastatic tissue architecture. Finally, validation in larger independent cohorts would help establish the reproducibility of the identified transcriptional patterns and clarify their potential relevance to metastatic heterogeneity.


### 8. Concluding synthesis of the Discussion
The combined findings of this study provide an integrated perspective on the transcriptional characteristics of breast cancer brain metastases, highlighting metabolic pathway enrichment, alterations in ECM-associated and immune-related transcription, and substantial cellular heterogeneity within metastatic specimens. The integration of bulk and single-nucleus transcriptomics revealed that tissue-level transcriptional differences and cellular compartment-specific expression patterns represent complementary aspects of metastatic biology. In particular, the coexistence of reduced bulk ECM-associated transcription and persistent ECM-associated expression within non-tumour cellular compartments underscores the importance of cellular context when interpreting transcriptomic alterations in metastatic tissues.

These observations support a model in which brain metastatic disease is associated with coordinated metabolic transcriptional changes and heterogeneous microenvironment-associated states rather than a single uniform molecular phenotype. Although the present analyses do not establish causal mechanisms or functional metabolic and extracellular changes, they provide a framework for further investigation of tumour-associated metabolic programs and cellular interactions within the brain metastatic microenvironment. Future studies combining patient-matched transcriptomic profiling, spatial analyses, functional metabolic characterization, and experimental validation may help clarify the biological mechanisms underlying these observations.
